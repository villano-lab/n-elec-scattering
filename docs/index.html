Neutron magnetic scattering backgrounds

A guided tour of the analysis behind the paper, one notebook at a time.

What this is about

At high recoil energies the magnetic scattering of ambient neutrons on atomic electrons is much rarer than their elastic scattering on nuclei. In the 1–2 keV range the order reverses, so a dark matter or other rare-event detector with a low enough threshold sees a neutron-electron background that must be accounted for. This repository holds the calculations: the neutron flux in the laboratory, the two scattering processes, the resulting recoil spectra, and their comparison with other backgrounds.

How the analysis fits together

  1. Contamination. The uranium and thorium content of the shotcrete and the norite rock around the cavern sets how many neutrons are made. Neutron flux → contamination.
  2. Neutron source. SOURCES4A turns the contamination into (α,n) and spontaneous-fission neutron spectra. Neutron flux → SOURCES4A.
  3. Flux in the cavern. A slab-source and shielding calculation gives the neutron flux in neutrons/cm²/s. Neutron flux → slab source.
  4. Scattering physics. Cross sections for neutron-nucleus elastic scattering (evaluated nuclear data) and for neutron-electron magnetic scattering. Scattering physics.
  5. Recoil spectra. The flux is convolved with the cross sections and combined over the natural isotopes of the detector materials. Neutron flux → convolution.
  6. Scaling and comparison. The flux is scaled to the shielded environments of published experiments and the neutron-electron rate is compared with other backgrounds. Final spectra, Comparisons.

Running the notebooks

conda env create -f environment.yml
conda activate nescatt_2025
jupyter lab

The notebooks use paths relative to their own folder, so start them from where they live. The SOURCES4A code itself is not in this repository; see the SOURCES README for how to obtain and run it.
